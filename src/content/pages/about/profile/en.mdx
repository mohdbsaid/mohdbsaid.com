My path into additive manufacturing started with a diploma in communications and electronics engineering — a foundation that still shapes how I approach every failed print: as a signal to trace back to its source, not a problem to route around. Over six years, that habit turned into a specialty — keeping FDM production lines dependable for government, energy, and industrial clients across Oman, through calibration, root-cause diagnostics, and workflow optimization that holds up under real production pressure, not just in a controlled test print.

Alongside that production work, I've spent a significant part of my career on the training side of the field — designing and delivering workshops that have reached more than 1,300 people across every governorate in Oman, and contributing to the development and commercialization of a locally built 3D printer. Both threads come from the same instinct: additive manufacturing in Oman is still young, and the more people who understand it properly, the better it gets for everyone working in it.
